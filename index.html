Keep the meaning in view

Symbols and concepts you will keep meeting.

A compact bridge into the papers. Check each source’s normalization and units before combining its formulas with another paper.

Order parameter
A quantity whose symmetry-changing expectation value characterizes a phase. Its amplitude, phase, wavevector and transformation under crystal symmetry matter.
Susceptibility χ
A response to an applied field. χ(q,ω) resolves momentum and frequency; χ(0,0), a local response and an NMR-weighted integral are different quantities.
χ″
The imaginary, dissipative part of a dynamical susceptibility. It tracks the ability to absorb energy from a time-dependent perturbation.
Hyperfine form factor A(q)
The momentum-dependent coupling between a nuclear spin and electronic spin density. It determines which electronic fluctuations that nucleus detects.
Knight shift K
A fractional NMR frequency shift. Its spin part is linked to a static magnetic response; orbital and quadrupolar contributions must be considered.
T₁ and 1/(T₁T)
T₁ is the nuclear spin-lattice relaxation time. Dividing its inverse by T changes the temperature dependence and aids comparison with dynamical response.
Pseudogap
A partial suppression of low-energy electronic spectral weight. The observation does not alone specify its microscopic cause or an order parameter.
CDW and SDW
Charge- and spin-density waves break translation symmetry with a modulation. Their wavevectors and spin/charge relation need to be specified.
Nematic
An electronic state that breaks an axis-interchange symmetry while preserving translations. Crystal anisotropy can act as an explicit field on it.
Quenched disorder
A fixed irregular potential or local environment during electronic equilibration. It can pin otherwise fluctuating correlations.
d-wave gap
A gap transforming in a d-like crystal-symmetry channel. For dₓ²₋ᵧ² on a square lattice it changes sign between x and y and has diagonal nodes.
Superfluid stiffness
The energy cost of spatially twisting the condensate phase. Pair formation and establishing phase coherence can have different scales.
Quantum-critical fan
A finite-temperature crossover regime influenced by a continuous zero-temperature transition, within a justified microscopic energy window.
Kondo-like scale
In Bobroff’s paper, a phenomenological scale inferred from local-susceptibility curvature. It is not by itself proof of every conventional Kondo signature.
