Change the probe, keep the host

A nucleus weights a spectrum.

Move a chosen host peak and compare a constant coupling with an ideal equal two-neighbor coupling. The toy functions are χ(q) = 1/[1 + ((q − Q)/0.38)²] and |A(q)|² = cos²(qa/2). The lattice spacing is set to one in the drawing.

At Q = π the equal two-neighbor filter cancels the central peak, but its finite width leaves other contributions.
  • Green solid: host peak
  • Orange dashed: coupling weight
  • Purple solid: weighted spectrum

Normalized teaching functions; no measured relaxation rate or complete material-specific hyperfine tensor. Real sites and neighboring chains need their own geometry.

A forward problem you can inspect

Two rates can make one smooth recovery.

Change a known two-component model: M(t) = 1 − w exp(−Rslow t) − (1−w) exp(−Rfast t). This is a saturation-style q = 1 teaching kernel, with no noise. It does not perform inverse-Laplace analysis.

Slow and fast exponential recoveries form a smooth weighted sum.
  • Green: slow recovery
  • Orange dashed: fast recovery
  • Purple: weighted sum

RCOG = exp[w ln Rslow + (1−w) ln Rfast] for these log-rate bin weights; the arithmetic rate mean is a different quantity. Nuclear preparation and pulse sequence determine the appropriate real kernel.
