4. From many transitions to plane and chain assignment

The assignment follows tensor symmetry and an NMR Hamiltonian, not just the zero-field frequency.

The research question: How does the experiment identify 31.5 MHz as plane Cu?

In two minutes

The whole guide at a glance

  1. A spin-3/2 nucleus has one central transition (m = ±½) and two satellites (±½ ↔ ±3/2).
  2. Two Cu sites × three field directions × three ⁶³Cu transitions give 18 lineshapes, plus ⁶⁵Cu central lines for comparison.
  3. The frequencies are fitted by diagonalizing the Hamiltonian, constraining the magnetic-shift and EFG tensors together.
  4. The c-special family matches plane Cu (31.5 MHz NQR) and the a-special shift family matches chain Cu (22 MHz); the chain EFG is strongly nonaxial.
  5. A zero-field NQR frequency alone cannot name a crystal axis; redundant transitions make the assignment a consistency test.

The whole argument in everyday language

First, see what this work is trying to explain.

The assignment follows tensor symmetry and an NMR Hamiltonian, not just the zero-field frequency. A spin 3/2 nucleus has a central transition between m=±1/2 and two satellite transitions involving m=±3/2. For each Cu site, the thesis measures the three⁶³Cu transitions for each of three crystal directions:18 lineshapes in total. Additional⁶⁵Cu central transitions supply comparisons. The narrow plane-Cu central line for H₀∥c can be excited across its approximately 25 kHz width and Fourier-transformed from the echo. Broader lines are measured point by point with tuned pulses. The underlying data are therefore collected differently according to bandwidth. The table and valence chapter use opposite overall EFG sign conventions; NQR magnitude and axial/nonaxial distinctions do not depend on that overall sign.

Terms to understand before starting
  • Plane Cu / chain Cu: the two crystallographic copper sites in YBCO
  • EFG: electric field gradient, which couples to a nuclear quadrupole moment
  • Magnetic shift: the field-proportional frequency change after quadrupole contributions are separated
  • W₁: the thesis’s elementary satellite population-transition rate, not automatically 1/T₁
  • Redfield contribution: echo decay generated by the fluctuating fields also responsible for population recovery
  • Transferred hyperfine coupling: a nuclear spin senses electronic spins through orbital mixing or intervening atoms
Central transition
The m = −1/2 to +1/2 nuclear transition of the spin-3/2 system.
Satellite transition
A transition connecting the central levels with m = ±3/2.
Tensor symmetry
Directional structure that helps distinguish plane and chain local environments.
Global EFG sign
Overall sign convention that can reverse components while leaving the NQR frequency unchanged.
Source convention
The specified rate, width or axis definition that must accompany numerical comparison with other sources.

Fit two tensors

Look for: Track the two contributions to each frequency.

The measured frequencies constrain the EFG and magnetic-shift tensors simultaneously. The thesis starts near the result of perturbation theory and diagonalizes the Cu Hamiltonian, adjusting tensor values to reproduce the observed transitions. The appendix supplies an exact diagonalization for field along a principal axis. This is useful because the satellite quadrupole splitting is substantial; the central transition also receives a second-order contribution when the relevant transverse EFG components differ. Part 1

The measured set is deliberately redundant

A spin 3/2 nucleus has a central transition between m=±1/2 and two satellite transitions involving m=±3/2. For each Cu site, the thesis measures the three⁶³Cu transitions for each of three crystal directions:18 lineshapes in total. Additional⁶⁵Cu central transitions supply comparisons. The narrow plane-Cu central line for H₀∥c can be excited across its approximately 25 kHz width and Fourier-transformed from the echo. Broader lines are measured point by point with tuned pulses. The underlying data are therefore collected differently according to bandwidth. One should compare positions and widths with awareness of excitation and echo timing, not assume every spectrum is a single identical digital acquisition.

Part 2

Fit the Hamiltonian before naming a shift

The measured frequencies constrain the EFG and magnetic-shift tensors simultaneously. The thesis starts near the result of perturbation theory and diagonalizes the Cu Hamiltonian, adjusting tensor values to reproduce the observed transitions. The appendix supplies an exact diagonalization for field along a principal axis. This is useful because the satellite quadrupole splitting is substantial; the central transition also receives a second-order contribution when the relevant transverse EFG components differ. The reported plane shift has Kaa≈Kbb≈0.59% and Kcc≈1.267%; the chain shift has Kaa≈1.38% and Kbb,Kcc≈0.55–0.60%. These contrasts concern magnetic shifts after the electric contribution has been separated. They are not simply comparisons of raw central frequencies.

Part 3

Symmetry identifies the families

Plane Cu’s approximate local symmetry about c matches the c-special shift and EFG family. Chain Cu’s shift is approximately symmetric about a, matching its local coordination. The chain EFG is strongly nonaxial, and the author treats that feature separately rather than asserting that every chain tensor is axial. With the site families assigned, the fitted EFG predicts zero-field NQR frequencies:31.5 MHz for planes and 22.0 MHz for chains. Because an EFG is traceless, the NQR expression gives the same frequency under permutation of principal labels. A zero-field frequency therefore lacks the orientation information that made the single-crystal assignment powerful. Twinned a/b domains account for the multiple in-plane peaks; some plane satellite a/b labels can be reversed without changing the robust site inference. A tin signal from solder is identified in a high-frequency spectrum and must not be mistaken for an additional Cu site. The source assignment is supported by the full transition family, not by selecting one convenient peak.

Part 4

Redundant transitions make the site assignment a consistency test

For one spin-3/2 isotope, the central and satellite transitions carry different quadrupole sensitivities. Combining those transitions across field directions and then adding the second isotope provides more constraints than one zero-field line. The thesis fits the magnetic-shift and EFG tensors together so that an apparently simple line displacement is not assigned to the wrong interaction. The resulting symmetry identifies the plane and chain families and connects them to their NQR frequencies. This is why a frequency label by itself is insufficient to establish which Cu site has been measured.

The fit also has a specific remaining ambiguity. In a twinned specimen, certain a/b satellite assignments can be exchanged while preserving the relevant set of frequencies. That does not erase the distinction between the approximately c-special plane environment and the differently oriented chain environment. Similarly, reversing the overall EFG sign leaves the NQR frequency invariant, so conflicting sign conventions should not be averaged into an invented signed tensor. The physically robust conclusions are the site family, axial or nonaxial structure and consistent zero-field connection. When comparing the tables with later interpretation chapters, preserve the convention as well as the number. The redundancy strengthens what is identifiable and makes the unresolved labels explicit instead of pretending every fitted component is independently unique.

Explain it back.

Try answering aloud, then open the explanation.

Why is a powder NQR frequency insufficient for this assignment?

It does not determine which crystal direction is special in the fitted tensor.

Why is the central frequency not always γH₀(1+K)?

A second-order quadrupole correction can remain when the transverse EFG components differ.

Which part of this unit is measured and which requires a model?

The assignment follows tensor symmetry and an NMR Hamiltonian, not just the zero-field frequency. The original-page observations supply constraints; the added explanation distinguishes the calibration, electronic account or visibility limit needed for interpretation.

Why do multiple transitions strengthen the site label?

Their different sensitivities jointly constrain magnetic and quadrupole tensors, so the site assignment must satisfy redundant symmetry evidence.
