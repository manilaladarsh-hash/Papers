6. Local distortion versus long-range order

The squared tilt can be finite even when opposite tilt directions cancel in a long-range average.

The research question: How can a locally tilted octahedron coexist with an average tetragonal lattice?

In two minutes

The whole guide at a glance

  1. Below a local temperature T_Q the Cu resonance centre changes curvature, sometimes where diffraction shows a lower onset or none at all.
  2. An EFG calculation links this change to tilts of the CuO₆ octahedra.
  3. Opposite local tilts can cancel in an average while each octahedron stays tilted: ⟨θ⟩ ≈ 0 but ⟨θ²⟩ > 0, and the NQR frequency responds to the latter.
  4. So NQR and diffraction can show different structural onsets without contradiction.
  5. For x = 0.04 below T_Q, extra width is assigned to a tilt distribution — conditional on a smooth continuation of the electronic width and independent mechanisms.

The whole argument in everyday language

First, see what this work is trying to explain.

The squared tilt can be finite even when opposite tilt directions cancel in a long-range average. The Cu resonance center changes curvature below a local temperature TQ, including compositions where long-range diffraction shows a lower structural onset or no comparable transition. Singer uses an EFG calculation to relate this change to orthorhombic distortions of CuO₆ octahedra. To do so, the thesis imports relationships among tilt angle, strain and oxygen geometry from long-range diffraction and assumes that comparable constraints apply locally. The resulting NQR shift is proportional to the sample average of the squared local tilt. Opposite signs therefore contribute equally. Local structural constraints are borrowed from long-range measurements and assumed applicable.

Terms to understand before starting
  • Nominal x: Sr atoms per LSCO formula unit, used as the average hole-doping label.
  • Electric field gradient (EFG): curvature of the electric potential at a nucleus; determines the Cu quadrupole resonance.
  • Knight shift: the nuclear resonance shift caused by electronic magnetic response, with orbital and spin contributions.
  • T₁ and T₂: recovery of longitudinal nuclear magnetization and decay of transverse coherence; they answer different questions.
  • Hyperfine form factor: the wavevector-dependent weighting through which a nucleus samples electronic spin fluctuations.
  • HWHM: half width at half maximum; the thesis also defines an effective width from the second moment.
Mean-square tilt
Average squared local distortion, which can remain finite while the signed mean cancels.
TQ
Local structural onset inferred under the source NQR model, distinct from long-range order.
Second moment
Variance-based spectral width used in the source statistical decomposition.
Independent width assumption
Combining separate squared widths assumes appropriate independent contributions and a baseline.
Source convention
The specified rate, width or axis definition that must accompany numerical comparison with other sources.

A descriptive explanation, step by step

Part 1

Separate local magnitude from alignment

The Cu resonance center changes curvature below a local temperature TQ, including compositions where long-range diffraction shows a lower structural onset or no comparable transition. Singer uses an EFG calculation to relate this change to orthorhombic distortions of CuO₆ octahedra. To do so, the thesis imports relationships among tilt angle, strain and oxygen geometry from long-range diffraction and assumes that comparable constraints apply locally. The resulting NQR shift is proportional to the sample average of the squared local tilt. Opposite signs therefore contribute equally. A population of positive and negative tilts can yield zero mean angle but a finite mean-square angle. Long-range alignment and local distortion magnitude are different observables, so a local NQR anomaly does not by itself establish coherent structural order over the entire sample.

Part 2

Read the fitted thermal parameters

The inferred mean-square tilt is parametrized below TQ by an amplitude and a reduced-temperature power. In the undoped compound its behavior is compatible with the long-range structural parameters. For doped samples the local onset can be higher and the thermal evolution more rounded. Singer interprets the combined local and diffraction results using both displacive changes in tilt magnitude and order–disorder changes in alignment. This is a physically useful distinction, but its quantitative parameters depend on the imported structural constraints and frequency baseline. The NQR fit is not an independent measurement of all atomic coordinates. In particular, the fact that NQR responds to θ² limits what can be said about the signs of tilts or their spatial correlations. The linked comparison of fitted local parameters with diffraction parameters should be read as evidence for different local and long-range behavior, not as a complete structural solution.

Part 3

The extra width has another ambiguity

For x = 0.04 below TQ, the full linewidth exceeds a proposed continuation of the random-donor and electronic-patch contribution. Singer assigns the remaining variance to a distribution of local tilt magnitudes. This requires assumptions: the electronic width continues smoothly, the added mechanisms are independent, and the extra structural width comes predominantly from tilt distributions. A trial distribution in θ² is adjusted to reproduce the line while maintaining the mean-square tilt inferred from the center. Those distributions are therefore conditional reconstructions rather than uniquely extracted measured probability densities. Adding widths in quadrature is appropriate to independent second-moment contributions, not a license to subtract arbitrary peak widths linearly. This unit supplies the reason to stop a naive Cu radius analysis below local structural onset and to seek the oxygen spin-channel cross-check.

Part 4

Mean tilt and mean-square tilt describe different physics

Opposite local tilts can cancel in an average even though each octahedron remains distorted. The mean tilt can then be near zero while the mean square is finite. NQR frequency responds to the latter within the source's expansion, so a local onset can appear before the distortions align into the long-range diffraction pattern. This explains why average tetragonal symmetry and a locally distorted environment are compatible instead of contradictory. The temperature fitted to that local response should not be renamed the long-range structural transition automatically.

The linewidth separation adds another inference. Combining widths through second moments assumes an appropriate statistical decomposition, including the continuation chosen for the electronic or random-donor part. A residual width is then assigned to a tilt distribution under that account. Different covariance or baseline assumptions could change the inferred tilt spread, so the subtraction is not an independent direct map of local angles. The source's reduced-temperature power describes its fitted thermal evolution; it does not prove one universal critical exponent or a sharp calibrated domain boundary. Keeping center shift, second-moment width, local magnitude and long-range alignment separate allows the structural evidence to inform the patch model without erasing either the electronic distribution or the approximation needed to distinguish the two.

Explain it back.

Try answering aloud, then open the explanation.

Can ⟨θ⟩ = 0 and ⟨θ²⟩ > 0 coexist?

Yes. Equal positive and negative local tilts cancel in their mean but add in their mean square.

Is a tilt probability distribution uniquely fixed by one width and one mean?

No. Additional distribution-shape and mechanism assumptions are needed.

Which part of this unit is measured and which requires a model?

The squared tilt can be finite even when opposite tilt directions cancel in a long-range average. The original-page observations supply constraints; the added explanation distinguishes the calibration, electronic account or visibility limit needed for interpretation.

Can the signed mean tilt vanish while mean-square tilt remains finite?

Yes. Opposite distortion directions can cancel in the mean while local magnitudes remain nonzero.
