7 · Cu NQR: singlet-like and paramagnetic regions

The observed Cu mixture is heterogeneous, and finite echo delay biases its inferred weights.

The research question: Why can an apparently simple average conceal a partially gapped response?

The whole argument in everyday language

First, see what this work is trying to explain.

The slow branch and the persisting fast branch are directly relevant because an intermediate characteristic rate can hide both. If the relative weights change on cooling, a stretched fit can move smoothly even though no individual environment follows that smooth average. The thesis’s two-component analysis instead asks which recoveries contribute and how much selected signal is associated with each. Its local-singlet account is one physical interpretation of the slow magnetic response, with an explicit distribution of gap-like scales.

Terms to understand before starting
  • Quantum spin liquid candidate: a material tested against a quantum-disordered model; a null ordering signal is insufficient identification.
  • K: local resonance shift, sensitive to a coupling-weighted static susceptibility.
  • 1/T₁: recovery rate that samples fluctuations in the probe frequency window.
  • ILT: constrained, regularized inference of a relaxation-rate distribution from recovery data.
  • log-rate density: probability is the area in d log₁₀(1/T₁), not peak height.
Quantum spin-liquid candidate
A material investigated for a nonconventional disordered quantum magnetic state; candidate evidence is not a unique phase identification.
Recovery kernel
The nuclear-state and preparation-dependent function mapping a relaxation rate to measured magnetization.
ILT
Inverse-Laplace-transform inference of rate-distribution weights from a measured recovery under a specified kernel and regularization.
Log-rate density
A distribution whose weight is integrated using dlog₁₀R rather than dR; peak height and integrated fraction differ.
Tikhonov regularization
Here, the positive term α ‖P‖² used to stabilize the nonnegative weight inference, with analysis-dependent broadening.
Geometric mean rate
The exponential of a normalized weighted mean of log rates; different from an arithmetic mean.
Echo visibility
The detected fraction remaining after transverse decay, pulse selection and timing.
Frequency–rate map
A joint representation connecting selected resonance frequency, distribution of rates and retained equilibrium intensity.
Affected neighborhood
The spins contributing to a nuclear response near a defect, without assuming an imaged sharp real-space boundary.

A descriptive explanation, step by step

Follow the full argument.

Part 1

Why use copper NQR

The Cu nucleus lies on the kagome spin site and strongly couples to its local environment. High-field powder spectra of spin-three-halves copper have a large orientation-dependent quadrupole complication. NQR probes the quadrupole transition without an applied static field and supplies a more practical recovery measurement. The source compares deuterated powder Zn-barlowite and herbertsmithite. Interlayer magnetic Cu defects are part of the real sample: the nominal material names must be distinguished from the reported nonideal compositions. Isotope peaks and bromine overlap also need correct assignment before attributing a signal to Cu dynamics.

Part 2

Read the split distributions before the characteristic rate

At higher temperatures, the inferred Cu rate distribution has a relatively narrow component near the paramagnetic scale, aside from the lattice-freezing region. Below roughly 30 K, a slower component develops while the faster component persists. The author fits the log-rate distributions with two Gaussian components and interprets the slow branch through local singlet formation with different excitation gaps. The characteristic stretched rate can move between branches as the weights change, even though it equals neither local component. Therefore an apparent power-law average does not establish that every Cu spin has the same gapless law, nor does a suppressed average establish a homogeneous singlet state.

Part 3

The fraction is bounded by visibility

The thesis obtains Fsinglet from the fitted component areas. Its low-temperature estimate is roughly one-half and does not exceed about 60% in the reported window. Yet finite echo spacing preferentially suppresses paramagnetic Cu nuclei with faster transverse decay. The observed slow fraction is therefore an upper-bound estimate of the true fraction under the stated selection. The comparison of 6, 15 and 30 µs echoes directly demonstrates this bias. A selected NQR-peak frequency also need not have the same mixture as the integrated full spectrum. Keep these limits attached to every quoted fraction and distinguish spatially varying gaps from a universal gap parameter for the entire sample.

Interpret a fraction through the echo-delay control

If the relative weights change on cooling, a stretched fit can move smoothly even though no individual environment follows that smooth average. The thesis’s two-component analysis instead asks which recoveries contribute and how much selected signal is associated with each. Its local-singlet account is one physical interpretation of the slow magnetic response, with an explicit distribution of gap-like scales.

Now include detection. The paramagnetic component has faster transverse decay under the relevant conditions. Increasing the echo delay suppresses it preferentially, so the measured slow fraction rises relative to the total surviving signal. The source’s6,15 and 30 µs comparison demonstrates the direction of this bias. Consequently the quoted singlet fraction is an upper-bound estimate within that experiment, rather than a full census of Cu sites. A peak-frequency measurement also restricts the ensemble before the recovery is recorded. These controls do not erase the observation of two types of dynamics; they bound how confidently their visible weights can become spatial population fractions. State the selected frequency and timing beside every fraction, and keep the homogeneous-gap and homogeneous-gapless alternatives distinct from the source’s heterogeneous interpretation.

Why is Fsinglet an upper bound here?

The paramagnetic component has faster transverse decay and is relatively suppressed by finite τ, making the observed slow fraction too large.

What does a persistent fast branch imply for a homogeneous gap claim?

The observed ensemble retains fluctuating paramagnetic environments, so one uniformly gapped local response does not describe the measured mixture.
