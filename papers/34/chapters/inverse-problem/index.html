3 · From recoveries to a log-rate distribution

ILT needs a forward kernel and a stable constrained inversion, even when peak count is left open.

The research question: What must be assumed to infer P(1/T₁)?

In two minutes

The whole guide at a glance

  1. A measured recovery is an integral over independently relaxing nuclei, written on a logarithmic rate axis because rates span decades.
  2. Weight lives in P(R) d log₁₀R: a tall narrow peak can hold less weight than a broad low one.
  3. Single, stretched or double exponentials assume a shape; the ILT instead minimizes residual + α‖P‖² with positivity.
  4. Too little α gives noise-driven spikes; too much broadens and merges peaks.
  5. Going forward again — rebuilding the recovery from the inferred weights — checks compatibility but cannot add information outside the measured delays.

The whole argument in everyday language

First, see what this work is trying to explain.

Imagine knowing every bin weight and rate. Summing their recoveries through the specified kernel gives a forward curve directly. Inverting that operation is harder because different rate histograms can produce curves that differ by less than the measurement noise at the sampled delays. Positivity removes negative weights but does not create new information. A finite logarithmic grid makes the problem computable while also fixing the fastest and slowest represented rates. Features outside that window can appear as missing preparation or boundary weights.

Terms to understand before starting
  • Quantum spin liquid candidate: a material tested against a quantum-disordered model; a null ordering signal is insufficient identification.
  • K: local resonance shift, sensitive to a coupling-weighted static susceptibility.
  • 1/T₁: recovery rate that samples fluctuations in the probe frequency window.
  • ILT: constrained, regularized inference of a relaxation-rate distribution from recovery data.
  • log-rate density: probability is the area in d log₁₀(1/T₁), not peak height.
Quantum spin-liquid candidate
A material investigated for a nonconventional disordered quantum magnetic state; candidate evidence is not a unique phase identification.
Recovery kernel
The nuclear-state and preparation-dependent function mapping a relaxation rate to measured magnetization.
ILT
Inverse-Laplace-transform inference of rate-distribution weights from a measured recovery under a specified kernel and regularization.
Log-rate density
A distribution whose weight is integrated using dlog₁₀R rather than dR; peak height and integrated fraction differ.
Tikhonov regularization
Here, the positive term α ‖P‖² used to stabilize the nonnegative weight inference, with analysis-dependent broadening.
Geometric mean rate
The exponential of a normalized weighted mean of log rates; different from an arithmetic mean.
Echo visibility
The detected fraction remaining after transverse decay, pulse selection and timing.
Frequency–rate map
A joint representation connecting selected resonance frequency, distribution of rates and retained equilibrium intensity.
Affected neighborhood
The spins contributing to a nuclear response near a defect, without assuming an imaged sharp real-space boundary.

Forward and inverse are different operations

Look for: Rates generate a recovery through the known kernel; inversion must contend with noisy incomplete information.

Forward calculation starts with known rates and weights and predicts a recovery. Inversion starts with sampled, noisy recovery and asks which distributions remain compatible with it. The second direction is not simply reading a hidden curve backward; finite delays and a broad kernel make it intrinsically less informative. The logarithmic rate grid supplies a finite computational window, not an infinitely resolved spectrum.

An inverse answer remains conditional on the acquisition.

A descriptive explanation, step by step

Part 1

The distribution has a measure

The measured recovery is an integral over the different independently relaxing nuclei. The thesis defines its density on a logarithmic rate axis because rates span orders of magnitude. Probability or signal weight belongs to P(R)d log₁₀R. A tall narrow peak can have less integrated weight than a broad lower peak. The source’s initial distribution integral equals equilibrium signal; normalized distributions are then used when discussing fractions. Distinguish that normalization from the raw frequency-dependent signal, which is necessary when assembling a two-dimensional map. Comparing areas after separately normalizing all frequencies to one would discard information about the line shape.

Part 2

Why fitting one rate can hide a second environment

A single exponential assumes one rate. A stretched exponential encodes a particular broad distribution through a characteristic rate and β, and reducing β broadens that implied family. Neither parameter is a universal average or a direct count of local environments. A double-exponential fit assumes two components beforehand. ILT instead represents a nonnegative set of log-rate bins and asks which weights reproduce the recovery under the stated kernel. This removes a prescribed peak count but retains independent relaxation, finite-rate support, a preparation factor and noise assumptions. The thesis’s sampling typically has fewer delay points than represented rate bins, making stable inversion a central issue rather than a numerical detail.

Part 3

What the Tikhonov term actually penalizes

The source minimizes a residual plus α‖P‖² subject to positivity. This is a penalty on the weight-vector norm, not an explicit derivative smoothing penalty. It tends to distribute weights rather than allow unstable isolated spikes; nevertheless the artificial broadening depends on discretization, α and signal quality. Small α can produce spikes that track noise, while large α can merge or smear components. The source illustrates both failures with known synthetic data. Treat recovered width as an analysis-dependent observation until its stability is checked. A good inverse result should be evaluated through forward reconstruction and changes in relevant acquisition and inversion choices.

Part 4

Read an inferred histogram as a conditional solution

Imagine knowing every bin weight and rate. Summing their recoveries through the specified kernel gives a forward curve directly. Inverting that operation is harder because different rate histograms can produce curves that differ by less than the measurement noise at the sampled delays. Positivity removes negative weights but does not create new information. A finite logarithmic grid makes the problem computable while also fixing the fastest and slowest represented rates. Features outside that window can appear as missing preparation or boundary weights.

The norm penalty chooses a stable compromise among data-compatible solutions. Its presence means recovered peak width contains numerical broadening as well as possible physical heterogeneity. This source uses α‖P‖², so it should not be explained as explicitly penalizing the derivative of P. Compare a forward reconstruction with the measured recovery, then inspect whether the meaningful components persist when reasonable analysis choices change. A smooth reconstruction is necessary but is not a uniqueness proof. The measure also matters: the plotted density is integrated in log rate, and a component’s area supplies its weight. Peak height alone cannot count its population. Keep normalized distribution shape separate from the equilibrium amplitude when combining frequencies into a map; otherwise a mathematically tidy normalization can erase the physical line intensity.

Translate the mathematical statement

Essential equations, meaning and assumptions

M = KP + E; P ≥ 0

Symbols and units: M: sampled recovery vector; K: transition-specific recovery kernel; P: nonnegative log-rate bin weights; E: noise.

Physical meaning: The rate histogram is inferred conditional on K, acquisition times and the represented rate range.

PDF p. 60

Pα = arg min(P ≥ 0) {‖M − KP‖² + α‖P‖²}

Symbols and units: α: positive regularization strength; ‖·‖: Euclidean norm.

Physical meaning: This source uses a norm penalty on the weights, not a derivative roughness penalty. Its artificial broadening must not be mistaken for a measured intrinsic width.

Explain it back.

Try answering aloud, then open the explanation.

What are the bin weights?

They are density times log-bin width, with a chosen total-signal or probability normalization. They are not density per linear rate.

Is ILT assumption-free?

No. It assumes a recovery kernel and represented window, positivity, independently relaxing populations and a way to stabilize noisy inversion.

What is the strongest supported conclusion of this unit?

ILT needs a forward kernel and a stable constrained inversion, even when peak count is left open. Density, weights and total intensity must be normalized consistently.
