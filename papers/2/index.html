Distinguishing Patterns of Charge Order - Stripes or Checkerboards

Four Fourier peaks or a checkerboard-looking image do not uniquely identify the clean-limit order when disorder pins short-range charge correlations.

The research question: Why a charge image can be ambiguous?

In two minutes

The whole guide at a glance

  1. A stripe modulates density along one direction; a checkerboard has two perpendicular modulations in the same place.
  2. Near an ordering instability, defects pin short-range modulations whose look depends mainly on the susceptibility, not on which clean order would win.
  3. So four Fourier peaks or a checkerboard-like image do not uniquely identify the clean-limit order: x and y stripe domains give the same peaks.
  4. Local filters give amplitudes A_x and A_y; their normalized imbalance η_orient helps, but only in a validated regime.
  5. Lattice context matters: in LTT neighbouring planes prefer orthogonal axes, so a tetragonal crystal can hold stripes in every plane.

The whole argument in everyday language

First, see what this work is trying to explain.

Four peaks in a Fourier map do not automatically mean a checkerboard. Separate stripe domains, different layers, or two waves sharing a region can all contribute both orientations. This paper constructs controlled density-wave models, adds disorder, and asks which image diagnostics can recover the underlying clean preference. When correlations persist over several periods, local envelope amplitudes and structural response can help. When defects induce only short patterns, stripe and checkerboard hypotheses can generate almost the same picture. The guide follows amplitude, phase, orientation and correlation length separately, then explains the limits of the preliminary tunnelling comparison. Its central lesson is how to make—and sometimes refuse—a symmetry inference from a disordered image.

Terms to understand before starting
  • Density wave: A spatial modulation of a density-like observable at a characteristic wavevector.
  • Complex amplitude: A slowly varying field retaining both the strength and phase of a modulation.
  • Quenched disorder: A fixed defect configuration while the electronic state equilibrates.
  • Commensurability: Locking of a modulation period to a rational relation with the lattice.

A descriptive explanation, step by step

Follow the full argument.

Read the full detailed argument8 parts · about 6 min

Part 1

1 · Define a stripe by symmetry, not appearance

A stripe is a unidirectional density modulation at one wavevector and its harmonics. A square-symmetric checkerboard contains two perpendicular modulations in the same region. Both break translation symmetry; the simple stripe additionally chooses an axis. The scalar density can represent an integrated local density of states, not exclusively the total electric charge density. A tunnelling image therefore needs a justified connection to the relevant electronic order.

Commensurate order is locked to a rational lattice period. Incommensurate order is not. Bond-centered and site-centered labels distinguish reflection symmetries for commensurate patterns; they are not generally distinct phases for an incommensurate wave. A global image can average stripe domains of both orientations and display four peaks, just as a bidirectional pattern can. Counting the peaks loses the information about whether both components coexist locally.

Part 2

2 · Read the two-order-parameter free energy

The complex fields φx and φy encode the amplitude and phase of modulations along the two axes. Their rapid spatial oscillations are factored out; gradients of the fields describe slow changes, domains and phase distortions. The quadratic coefficient α controls the clean mean-field tendency to order. For α < 0, positive γ penalizes simultaneous x and y amplitudes and favors stripes; negative γ favors both amplitudes and hence checkerboards. This model assumes square symmetry, weak gradients and negligible commensurability effects.

Disorder enters as a fixed potential coupled to the density. It pins both translational phase and, indirectly, orientational patterns. The simulated images are obtained by minimizing this Landau–Ginzburg functional for specified impurity configurations. They are model-generated examples, not experimental STM photographs. Quenched disorder means that its configuration is held fixed during the electronic equilibration.

Part 3

3 · Why a symmetric state can look ordered

For positive α the clean state is symmetric, but its susceptibility can be large near an instability. A weak defect then induces a local modulation. To leading order that response depends on the quadratic susceptibility, not on γ, the coefficient that chooses stripes or checkerboards after ordering. Consequently the two clean preferences can yield remarkably similar pinned images. The right panels of Figures 1 and 2 are the key controlled comparison.

When correlation length is only one or two modulation periods, the paper finds no robust way to infer the clean-limit pattern from appearance alone. Long, weakly disordered domains are more diagnostic. This is an inverse problem: many underlying states can map to similar measurements. A good analysis must report where its inference becomes ambiguous, rather than giving every attractive real-space image a definitive symmetry label.

Part 4

4 · What the filters measure and when they work

The authors apply local filters around the two ordering wavevectors to obtain slowly varying amplitudes Ax and Ay. The CDW measure tracks translational coherence; the orientational measure tracks differences in the squared amplitudes. ηorient is invariant under an overall rescaling of intensity, which helps when unknown tunnelling matrix elements prevent absolute calibration. A pure single-axis pattern approaches one; an equal-amplitude checkerboard approaches zero in the clean ordered limit.

The threshold near ηorient = 0.2 in their figures is a result of the specified simulations and disorder averaging, not a universal experimental classifier. Positive-α pinned patterns can sit near that value regardless of γ. Likewise an orientational correlation length longer than a CDW correlation length can be suggestive without proving an orientational glass. Part 5

5 · Why crystal structure and complementary probes matter

An orthorhombic distortion acts differently on stripes and bidirectional order, and commensurate locking restricts wavevector shifts. In an LTT structure successive CuO₂ planes have opposite preferred axes even though the full three-dimensional crystal is classified as tetragonal. Plane-specific anisotropy can thus generate both global peak orientations without local checkerboard order. Mixed spin-wavevector combinations, peak splitting and response to distortion provide additional tests, subject to resolution and coupling assumptions.

The 214-family scattering evidence is the strongest part of their stripe interpretation. The short-range BSCCO STM example is more tentative: one image and a small translational coherence length cannot substitute for a statistical ensemble or an independently established clean ordered phase. The authors favor stripe tendencies overall while acknowledging ambiguous cases. Apply the symmetry reasoning to a new sample, but do not import the historical conclusion indiscriminately to every modern cuprate charge-order measurement.

Part 6

6 · The amplitude and the phase carry different information

A density wave has a wavelength, an amplitude and a phase. The wavelength sets the spacing between repeated features; the amplitude sets their contrast; the phase sets where those features fall relative to the origin. Moving a stripe pattern by a fraction of its wavelength can change the phase without changing its orientation or strength. This is why translational disorder and orientational disorder require separate diagnostics. A region can keep a preferred axis after neighboring regions have lost a common position for their density-wave maxima.

The complex fields φx and φy are designed to keep that information. Their rapidly oscillating exponential factors place the peaks at the ordering vectors, while their slowly varying envelopes describe local distortions. Local filters extract those envelopes from an image. They do not simply threshold every bright pixel and declare it a stripe. Filtering involves a chosen spatial and momentum window, which trades local resolution against wavevector selectivity. The source explains its filter size in relation to the modulation period, so reproducing the method requires that choice as well as the final formula.

The orientational imbalance compares squared envelope amplitudes. It is insensitive to a global multiplication of the image intensity, an advantage when the tunnelling matrix element is unknown. It remains sensitive to how the local windows are chosen and to whether the image actually represents the assumed order. Normalization solves one calibration problem; it does not remove all measurement assumptions.

Part 7

7 · Separate finding correlations from identifying the clean state

The experiment supplies a disordered image, but the scientific question asks what pattern the clean system would prefer. Those are different objects. In the ordered regime, disorder fragments a state that would otherwise remain coherent; increasing disorder shrinks domains. In the symmetric regime, the same defects induce a response that would disappear in their absence. A visible pattern therefore need not mean the defect-free system has already crossed an ordering transition.

The weak-defect expansion is especially instructive. Its leading susceptibility depends on the quadratic distance from the instability, while the quartic coefficient choosing stripe versus checkerboard preference enters only at higher order. The two hypotheses can therefore produce almost indistinguishable leading images. Adding more contrast is not automatically more information: once higher-order effects become large enough to reveal the preference, disorder may also have become strong enough to scramble the diagnostic.

The numerical tests use specified finite fields of view and average multiple disorder realizations for the statistical diagnostics. An actual experimental field of view is one realization, and its fluctuations need not equal the ensemble mean. The preliminary BSCCO comparison therefore supports a cautious statement about short-range tendencies rather than an exact fitted value of every clean Hamiltonian coefficient. The strongest transferable result is the identification of regimes where the inverse problem is informative and where it is fundamentally ambiguous.

Part 8

8 · Add lattice and scattering information to the image

The source proposes using a small orthorhombic distortion as an additional constraint because it couples differently to the two order components. A rectangular distortion selects a preferred original lattice axis; a diagonal distortion can rotate an incommensurate ordering vector. A checkerboard can become unequal along its two directions without becoming identical to a collection of single-axis domains. Commensurate locking restricts the possible shifts, so the response also tests whether the density wave follows the lattice or has an independently adjustable wavelength.

An LTT crystal is a particularly useful warning. Its overall symmetry is tetragonal, but consecutive planes have opposite preferred axes. Summing scattering from the planes can show both orientations even if each plane contains stripes. The unit cell and layer assignment are part of the interpretation, not decorative crystallographic detail. The source also uses the relation between charge and spin wavevectors to propose additional mixed-wavevector tests of simultaneous bidirectional order.

Applying any such diagnostic requires sufficient resolution and a stated coupling model. The absence of a small peak splitting is informative only when the expected splitting could actually have been detected. This is why the argument combines symmetry, correlation length, structural response and complementary scattering. An image alone can remain ambiguous while the larger evidence set favors a particular hypothesis.

Translate the mathematical statement

Essential equations, meaning and assumptions

ρ(r) = ρ̄ + [φₓ(r) exp(iQx) + φᵧ(r) exp(iQy) + c.c.]

Symbols and units: The two complex amplitudes encode separate orientations. Here Q is the magnitude of the ordering wavevector, and x and y are coordinates. c.c. ensures a real scalar density; φ varies slowly relative to the modulation period.

Physical meaning: The rapid exponentials set the modulation period. Complex amplitudes retain its local strength and position; their phases distinguish displaced waves even when the orientation is identical.

PDF p. 2

ηorient = ∫(|Ax|² − |Ay|²)² / ∫(|Ax|² + |Ay|²)²

Symbols and units: A normalized intensity-imbalance measure; its interpretation depends on regime, filtering and disorder, rather than only its numerical value.

Physical meaning: The numerator vanishes for equal local amplitudes and equals the denominator for a pure single-axis pattern. Both scale together under a global intensity change. Disorder and filtering determine whether this number identifies the clean tendency.

What the evidence supports—and where it stops

Explain it back.

Try answering aloud, then open the explanation.

Why do four charge-order peaks not prove a checkerboard?

Two sets of unidirectional domains, or alternating layer orientations, can produce the same global peak count.

Why is the weak pinned response insensitive to stripe-versus-checkerboard preference?

At leading order it depends on the quadratic susceptibility; the preference enters through higher-order interaction terms.

Should η = 0.21 always be called stripe order?

No. The quoted threshold is model- and regime-dependent, especially unreliable for short-range pinned fluctuations.

What changes when a wave shifts in space without turning?

Its translational phase changes, while its orientation and possibly its amplitude remain the same. This is why phase disorder and orientational disorder require separate measures.

Why can weak disorder fail to reveal the sign of γ?

The leading induced response depends on the quadratic susceptibility. The coefficient selecting the ordered stripe/checkerboard preference enters at higher order.

Why can an LTT specimen give both global orientations?

Successive planes prefer opposite axes. Summed scattering can therefore contain both orientations while each plane remains locally unidirectional.
