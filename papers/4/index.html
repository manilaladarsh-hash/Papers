Stripe order in superconducting La₂₋ₓBaₓCuO₄ (0.095 ≤ x ≤ 0.155)

Charge stripes occur throughout the measured doping range, including bulk superconductors, and are strongest near one eighth; the authors infer competition mainly with phase coherence.

The research question: Does static stripe order necessarily eliminate superconducting pairing?

The whole argument in everyday language

First, see what this work is trying to explain.

The study compares structure, charge stripes, spin stripes and superconductivity on pieces of the same LBCO crystals. Charge and spin order are strongest near one-eighth doping, but weak charge-order peaks also survive where bulk superconductivity is substantial. Several onset temperatures must be kept distinct: neutron elastic scattering admits slow fluctuations, genuinely static spin order appears lower, and weak in-plane superconducting correlations can precede bulk coherence. The exceptional lowest- and highest-doping specimens do not follow every typical onset relation. No clear loss of stripe intensity at bulk Tc is resolved. The authors consequently favor pairing compatible with stripes but competition over three-dimensional coherence, with that final mechanism remaining an interpretation rather than a direct pair-order measurement.

Terms to understand before starting
  • charge-order satellite: weak diffraction reflection from an associated lattice modulation
  • spin-order satellite: magnetic diffraction at an incommensurate wavevector
  • correlation length: size of correlated domains inferred from resolution-corrected widths
Charge satellite
A weak diffraction peak associated with a periodic charge-linked lattice modulation.
Incommensurability
Offset of an ordering wavevector from a commensurate reference.
Correlation length
Spatial range of ordered correlations inferred from momentum width with resolution checks.
Elastic window
Finite energy interval accepted as elastic scattering.
FC/ZFC
Field-cooled and zero-field-cooled magnetic measurement protocols.
LTLO
Less-orthorhombic intermediate tilted structure.
Reentrant response
A response that appears, weakens or disappears, then returns on further cooling.
Phase coherence
A consistent superconducting phase relation across regions or layers.

A descriptive explanation, step by step

Follow the full argument.

Part 1

The same-sample design

Hücker and colleagues combine high-energy x-ray diffraction, neutron diffraction and static magnetization on LBCO single crystals spanning nominal x = 0.095–0.155. Pieces from the same crystals are essential because actual Ba content can differ slightly from the growth target. Charge-order diffraction detects the lattice modulation associated with electronic stripes; earlier resonant scattering provides separate evidence for electron-density modulation. Spin satellites are measured by neutrons, while magnetization follows structural signatures, spin-related anomalies and superconducting response. All reciprocal coordinates use the HTT cell, even when the real-space low-temperature cell is doubled and rotated. This convention makes charge offsets 2δ and magnetic offsets δ comparable without confusing cell labels. Oxygen-controlled growth and composition calibration remain important assumptions in the comparison.

Part 2

How strong and extensive the stripes are

Charge-order peaks are present at both endpoints as well as near one eighth. Their intensity and correlation length peak strongly around x = 0.125 and fall rapidly away from it. The weak endpoint signals therefore establish coexistence with bulk superconductivity without implying identical stripe strength everywhere. Resolution-corrected transverse scans are used for correlation lengths; longitudinal instrumental resolution is poorer. Spin order is also strongest near one eighth, with longer correlations along the spin stripes. No spin peak is resolved at x = 0.155, where charge order is very weak, so absence of detection is not an unlimited bound on all magnetic correlations. Charge incommensurability shifts with doping, but the simple δ = x relation is only approximate and improves after estimating actual Ba content.

Part 3

Structure and stacking constrain the onset

For most studied crystals, charge order begins at the transition from LTO into LTT or LTLO. At x = 0.155 the charge onset lies below the structural transition and the sample contains an LTT/LTLO mixture, making the phase-specific contribution unquantifiable. The measured interlayer charge-order modulation retains the same stacking type for x = 0.095–0.135: neighboring stripes rotate direction and parallel stripes in alternate layers are shifted. In selected samples, in-plane and out-of-plane charge correlations disappear together, contrasting with earlier reports of premature stacking loss. The paper does not resolve that discrepancy universally. Structural pinning helps stabilize stripes, but pressure results discussed by the authors show that long-range LTT is not a necessary condition for every stripe state.

Part 4

Static spin order and superconducting scales

The onset of neutron elastic spin intensity is higher than the scale assigned to truly static spin order because finite energy resolution admits slow critical fluctuations. Peak-width changes, weak magnetic susceptibility features and external μSR comparisons give lower static scales. Weak c-axis diamagnetism appears near that scale for compositions where it can be resolved, interpreted as in-plane superconducting correlations above bulk Tc. The magnetic contribution is extremely small and is not a full Meissner fraction. Bulk Tc follows a lower scale with a deep one-eighth depression. At x = 0.095, superconductivity initially develops in LTO, weakens during the structural/charge transition and reappears later. These distinctions rule out collapsing every onset into a single universally defined superconducting temperature.

Part 5

Competition, coexistence and quantitative limits

Where data quality permits, neither charge nor spin peak intensity shows a significant reduction when bulk superconductivity starts. Charge order also shows no clear change at the spin-static onset. The authors infer that stripes are compatible with local pairing but compete with three-dimensional phase coherence. That is a physically motivated interpretation, not a direct measurement of pair amplitude or unique proof of a pair-density wave. Their field-cooled and zero-field-cooled superconducting curves are normalized because flux pinning, defects and specimen geometry prevent an absolute volume comparison; the reference-section footnote states this explicitly. An empirical HTT transition calibration estimates actual x′ and improves agreement between studies, assuming stoichiometric oxygen. The source establishes broad coexistence and several discriminating constraints while retaining sample and model limits. Treat its calibrated compositions as estimates with assumptions rather than exact independently assayed values.

Part 6

What the charge diffraction peak measures

The high-energy nonresonant x-rays used here mainly reveal an atomic displacement modulation associated with charge order. The electronic interpretation also relies on earlier resonant evidence cited separately. This distinction does not make the measured peak unimportant: its position, width, intensity and temperature dependence are reproducible properties of the stripe-associated modulation. It does mean that one should not describe every count as a direct count of doped holes on a stripe. A scattering vector written in reciprocal-lattice units is another convention to keep visible. The authors use the HTT coordinates even when the physical low-temperature cell is rotated and doubled. With that common reference, charge offsets2delta and magnetic offsets delta can be compared without confusing an electronic modulation with a change of crystallographic indexing.

Part 7

Peak strength and correlation range are different measurements

A higher peak can result from a stronger modulation, a greater ordered amount or a narrower distribution in momentum. Its width provides complementary information about how far the correlations extend, after instrumental resolution is considered. The source therefore compares both intensity and correlation length across doping. Near one-eighth, charge and spin order are strongest and most spatially extended. Away from it, much weaker charge peaks still establish order within sensitivity, even when they cannot support the same quantitative precision as the strongest sample. A missing resolved spin peak at x=0.155 gives a detection limit rather than proof that all spin correlations vanish. The direction of each scan also matters: transverse and longitudinal instrumental resolutions are different, so widths should not be compared as if every geometry has the same resolving power.

Part 8

Layer registration is another part of stripe order

A two-dimensional stripe drawing omits how the pattern is placed in neighboring planes. The LBCO pattern rotates its preferred stripe direction between adjacent layers and shifts parallel stripes in alternate layers. The out-of-plane modulation therefore has a characteristic reciprocal-space dependence. The source finds no qualitative change in this stacking type for the compared x=0.095–0.135 samples. In selected measurements, in-plane and out-of-plane charge correlations disappear together on warming, rather than showing a universally earlier stacking loss. This comparison matters because an in-plane satellite alone cannot establish coherent registration through the crystal. It also explains the source’s careful discussion of differences with earlier reports. The present result is a specimen- and measurement-based constraint, not a declaration that every stripe sample must melt identically.

Part 9

Elastic depends on the instrument’s clock

An elastic neutron channel has a finite energy-resolution window. Fluctuations that become slower than that window can contribute intensity even while they are not static on a much longer timescale. The onset of measured elastic spin scattering can consequently lie above a scale associated with genuinely static order. The paper compares intensity onset, width changes, weak susceptibility features and external muon results to distinguish these scales. This is not disagreement between four measurements of one ideally defined number; they sample different aspects and clocks of the developing state. Nuclear magnetic probes in other guides introduce still other frequency and time windows. Use the original symbols and criteria in Fig. 11 before drawing one magnetic boundary from a phase-diagram label.

Part 10

Two endpoint samples test the usual sequence

At x=0.095, superconductivity begins while the crystal is still in LTO. Its response weakens as the lower structural and charge transition develops, then returns at lower temperature. This reentrant behavior is a concrete example of competing scales rather than a monotonic disappearance of pairing. At x=0.155, charge onset falls below the structural transition and the structure contains LTT and LTLO contributions. The paper cannot uniquely assign the weak charge peak to one structural fraction in that mixture. These endpoints should not be averaged away when stating that charge order usually begins at the lower tilt transition. Their exceptions constrain the role of structural pinning and show why a convenient common sequence is not a universal law across the entire composition range.

Part 11

Test competition at the transition rather than assuming it

If bulk superconductivity directly displaced the measured stripe order, one might expect a resolved decrease of charge or spin peak intensity when bulk Tc is crossed. Where data quality allows the comparison, the source does not see that decrease. The authors use this result to argue that charge stripes can be compatible with local pairing while hindering three-dimensional phase coherence. This reasoning is stronger than inferring competition solely because one-eighth has a low bulk Tc. It remains conditional: a change smaller than measurement sensitivity can be missed, and the experiment does not directly measure pairing amplitude or the sign of a pair-density wave. The useful scientific statement combines coexistence evidence with a proposed division between pairing and coherence, while preserving the missing direct order-parameter test.

Part 12

Sample calibration and magnetization normalization matter

The same-crystal design reduces comparisons between slightly different specimens, but nominal Ba still differs from estimated actual concentration. An empirical HTT transition relation is used to infer x-prime under a stoichiometric-oxygen assumption and improves inter-study agreement. It is a useful calibration, not a direct independent Ba assay. Superconducting FC and ZFC curves are normalized because pinning, defects and geometry prevent their amplitudes from serving as absolute volume comparisons. In particular, a normalized weak signal can look visually as large as a stronger one. Read the methodological footnotes as part of the experiment. They explain what the plots can compare reliably: transition shapes and temperatures, with explicit qualifications about composition and absolute superconducting amount.

What the evidence supports—and where it stops

  • Charge satellites measure associated lattice displacement; electronic modulation evidence is cited from separate resonant work.
  • x = 0.155 has weak signals and mixed structure; phase-specific coexistence is unresolved.
  • Calibration of x′ assumes stoichiometric oxygen.
  • Coherence competition is an interpretation; no unique PDW or local pairing-amplitude measurement is supplied.

Explain it back.

Try answering aloud, then open the explanation.

Why is neutron elastic onset not necessarily truly static onset?

Finite energy resolution includes sufficiently slow fluctuations before they freeze on slower probe timescales.

Does a normalized FC amplitude measure volume?

No. Pinning, geometry and sample quality motivated normalization, as explained in the paper's footnote.

Does structural pinning prove the lattice causes all stripes?

No. Pinning stabilizes a pattern; discussed pressure evidence allows stripes without long-range LTT.

Does the nonresonant x-ray peak directly count stripe holes?

It measures the stripe-associated atomic modulation; separate cited resonant evidence supports the electronic-density interpretation.

Why can neutron onset precede truly static spin order?

Finite energy resolution admits slowly fluctuating correlations into the elastic channel.

Why keep x=0.095 and0.155 separate?

They show reentrant superconducting response and delayed charge onset in a mixed structure, respectively.

What must be considered before converting a peak width to length?

Instrumental resolution and scan direction, as well as the assumed line-shape model.

Does no downturn at Tc uniquely prove PDW order?

No. It constrains direct displacement of stripe order and supports a coherence interpretation within sensitivity, without measuring the full pair order parameter.
