Differentiated roles of Lifshitz transition on thermodynamics and superconductivity in La2-xSrxCuO4

ARPES-derived three-dimensional bands account for much of the LSCO specific-heat maximum near a Lifshitz transition, while the d-wave gap evolves smoothly through it.

The research question: Does a Fermi-surface topology change explain the thermodynamic peak and enhance superconductivity?

In two minutes

The whole guide at a glance

  1. ARPES on 13.3 nm LSCO films from x ≈ 0.06 to 0.35, with doping fixed by the Luttinger count, maps bands and gaps across the Lifshitz transition.
  2. The Fermi surface changes from hole-like to electron-like when the chemical potential crosses the antinodal saddle — topology changes without broken symmetry.
  3. Photon-energy scans give a small interlayer hopping (t_z ≈ 0.03 t₁), placing the change between x = 0.206 and 0.214.
  4. The resulting 3D band density of states reproduces the location and much of the size of the specific-heat γ peak near x ≈ 0.21.
  5. The d-wave gap evolves smoothly through the transition with no enhancement there: a density-of-states maximum does not maximize pairing.

The whole argument in everyday language

First, see what this work is trying to explain.

ARPES-derived three-dimensional bands account for much of the LSCO specific-heat maximum near a Lifshitz transition, while the d-wave gap evolves smoothly through it. A Lifshitz transition occurs when the chemical potential crosses an antinodal saddle point and the large Fermi surface changes from hole-like to electron-like. This can enhance DOS without requiring symmetry breaking. The paper asks separately whether that enhancement accounts for a normal-state electronic heat-capacity peak and whether it controls superconducting gap size or symmetry. These questions need not have the same answer. The analysis concerns LSCO, and does not remove the possibility of different contributions in Nd- or Eu-substituted systems.

Terms to understand before starting
  • Fermi contour: constant-energy states at the chemical potential
  • Lifshitz transition: a change in Fermi-surface topology
  • Electronic density of states connects quasiparticle bands to entropy
ARPES
Angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy probing occupied dispersion and electronic spectra.
Lifshitz transition
Change of Fermi-surface topology as a band extremum or saddle crosses the chemical potential.
Saddle point
Band region with opposite curvature directions, capable of enhancing density of states.
kz dispersion
Variation of energy with momentum perpendicular to the conducting planes.
Luttinger count
Carrier-filling calibration inferred from an assumed relation to Fermi-surface volume.
Antinode
Momentum region where a d-wave gap is large; distinct from its node.
Detection limit
Bound set by sensitivity or resolution; an unresolved value is not necessarily exactly zero.

Calculate the thermal consequence

Why kz and doping calibration matter

Part 3

The band model and the finite transition interval

A three-dimensional tight-binding form includes first-, second- and third-neighbor in-plane hopping and a stacking-dependent interlayer term. The nodal velocity motivates t1 = 190 ± 15 meV and t3/t2 = −0.5; t2 and chemical potential vary with doping. Fitted tz is about 0.030t1 at x = 0.22 and 0.039t1 at x = 0.35. Using tz = 0.03t1 and interpolation, the source estimates the topology change between x = 0.206 and 0.214, with doping uncertainty about 0.01. Different kz slices can have different contour topology in that interval. The decimal bounds should therefore not be treated as exact universal doping endpoints. Correlation-renormalized spectroscopic bands are used, rather than an unmodified free-electron model.

Part 4

Thermodynamic agreement and its qualifications

The calculated DOS enters the Fermi entropy, whose derivative gives electronic C/T. Finite three-dimensionality turns the ideal two-dimensional van Hove divergence into a finite sharp peak. Updated small tz and doping-dependent in-plane parameters reproduce the location and much of the magnitude of the measured LSCO γ maximum near x = 0.21. At low doping, an arc restriction is added; farther overdoped, calculated γ is about 30 percent below thermodynamic values. The model neglects the transport-derived disorder broadening in its primary comparison, motivated by similar thermodynamic γ with and without Zn despite different resistivities. This is a substantive assumption: transport scattering and thermodynamic DOS need not have identical disorder sensitivity. The fit argues that a dominant extra quantum-critical contribution is not needed for this LSCO peak; it does not prove all quantum fluctuations absent.

Part 5

Why superconductivity behaves differently

Symmetrized spectra are fitted with a gap self-energy and instrumental convolution. At x = 0.17, 0.22 and 0.24, gap size follows the d-wave momentum form across the topology transition, with no special enhancement near the DOS maximum. At x = 0.12 the antinodal gap exceeds a nodal d-wave extrapolation, consistent with a distinct pseudogap contribution. For x at least 0.26 the gap lies below the experiment’s detectable limit; that is a resolution statement, not automatically proof of exactly zero gap. The result challenges a simple van-Hove-enhanced pairing explanation in this material. The online supporting information, including detailed growth and uncertainty checks, is referenced but is not part of the supplied seven-page PDF. Keep those absent details separate from the directly read main article.

Part 6

How to compare the evidence

The toy contour and the measured doping sequence must be distinguished. In a conventional electron-band picture, increasing chemical potential fills more electron states; adding holes runs in the opposite direction. The schematic compares the two sides of a saddle without using its left-to-right direction as a calibrated doping axis. In the source, actual Fermi volumes and photon-energy-dependent dispersion constrain the LSCO doping and interlayer parameters. The thermodynamic calculation then uses those renormalized bands, rather than inferring a divergence from an ideal two-dimensional cartoon. Its primary success is explaining much of a finite heat-capacity peak, with remaining overdoped discrepancy and disorder assumptions stated. The gap comparison supplies an independent response: a peak in accessible states does not automatically increase the superconducting interaction or gap.

Part 7

A topology transition changes connectivity rather than symmetry

The Fermi surface is the set of momenta whose quasiparticle energy equals the chemical potential. Near a saddle point, changing that chemical potential can alter the contour's connectivity or whether it encloses hole-like versus electron-like states. This is a Lifshitz change. It need not introduce an order parameter or break an additional crystal symmetry. The associated density of states can nevertheless change sharply because many states accumulate near the saddle energy. The supplied paper tests the thermodynamic consequences of that band feature separately from its superconducting consequences.

A strictly two-dimensional ideal band and a weakly three-dimensional band differ importantly. Finite out-of-plane dispersion means that different kz slices cross the saddle at different fillings. The resulting transition spans an interval, and thermal averaging further smooths the observable response. A narrowly quoted doping range should therefore be read with calibration uncertainty and the band assumptions attached. A teaching contour can show connectivity, but its hopping values, chemical potential and direction of motion must not be substituted for the article's fitted dispersion or identified automatically with increasing hole doping. The paper's actual evidence comes from spectroscopy and its constrained band fit.

Part 8

Connect dispersion, occupation and thermal response

Photoemission provides information about occupied electronic dispersions and excitation spectra. To calculate a heat coefficient, the article constructs a quasiparticle density of states from a band model, applies Fermi occupations and computes entropy. Differentiating entropy with temperature gives the electronic heat capacity. States close enough to the chemical potential to change occupation thermally dominate the low-temperature result. A saddle-related DOS enhancement can therefore raise C/T without requiring that the pairing interaction become stronger at the same composition.

This calculation has assumptions beyond the measured dispersion. Correlation renormalization, three-dimensional hopping, scattering broadening and the use of coherent states affect the inferred DOS. A comparison with independent calorimetry is an important constraint because it tests a different observable, but agreement does not uniquely exclude every additional many-body contribution. The superconducting gap is measured and analyzed separately, with its own spectral fit and detection limit. A smooth d-wave gap through a heat-capacity maximum is therefore a useful distinction between thermodynamics and pairing. An unresolved small gap should be described as below sensitivity rather than assigned an exact value of zero.

Part 9

The third dimension changes a singularity into a bounded peak

In an ideal two-dimensional model, a saddle can generate a particularly sharp DOS enhancement. The measured antinodal out-of-plane dispersion prevents treating the real specimen as exactly that idealization. Different kz slices cross the saddle at different fillings, broadening the topology change into a narrow interval and regularizing the thermal peak. Photon-energy scans provide the experimental constraint on this extra hopping rather than leaving it as an arbitrary smoothing knob. The small nodal dispersion and larger antinodal modulation also show why a single uniform three-dimensional correction would miss the momentum dependence.

The stated numerical interval remains subject to doping calibration and band interpolation uncertainty. Its decimal precision should not be mistaken for a universal boundary known more accurately than the quoted uncertainty. The source uses a Luttinger-based Fermi-volume count and checks a single-crystal benchmark to support comparison between films and bulk thermodynamics. That benchmark strengthens the particular band comparison, but does not prove that every film property equals every bulk specimen. The online supporting information is absent from the supplied main article and must not be presented as independently reviewed evidence for further details.

Part 10

Two experimental questions receive different answers

The first question is whether the band DOS can account for the location and much of the magnitude of the LSCO normal-state heat peak. The constrained calculation does substantially so under its stated assumptions, with residual discrepancies and neglected transport-like disorder broadening still acknowledged. The second question is whether superconducting gap magnitude or symmetry is specially enhanced at the topology change. The measured superconducting films retain the d-wave relation across it without a corresponding special gap maximum. Combining these results shows that a large thermal response and a stronger pairing scale are not synonymous observables.

The underdoped antinodal deviation adds another qualification: there the antinodal gap exceeds a simple extrapolation of the near-nodal d-wave behavior, so even one momentum-dependent gap plot contains more than a single uncomplicated superconducting scale. At larger doping, an unresolved gap is bounded by sensitivity rather than proved to be identically zero. The supplied article therefore provides a material-specific band explanation of a thermodynamic feature and a separate experimental constraint on pairing. It does not universally rule out quantum fluctuations or replace every cuprate heat anomaly with the same saddle mechanism.

Essential equations, meaning and assumptions

γ = Cel/T = ∂S/∂T

Symbols and units: γ: Sommerfeld-type coefficient; Cel: electronic heat capacity; S: electronic entropy.

Physical meaning: The entropy is computed from a quasiparticle DOS; the comparison is model dependent.

PDF p. 4

S = −kB ∫ [f ln f + (1−f) ln(1−f)] ρ(E) dE

Symbols and units: f: Fermi occupation; ρ(E): electronic DOS; kB: Boltzmann constant.

Physical meaning: States within a thermal window near EF contribute to entropy; an enhanced DOS can increase γ without stronger pairing.

Δ(k) ∝ |cos(kxa) − cos(kya)|/2

Symbols and units: Δ: gap magnitude; kx, ky: momenta; a: in-plane lattice constant.

Physical meaning: The tested d-wave angular dependence persists on both sides of the Lifshitz range in the measured superconducting films.

Explain it back.

Try answering aloud, then open the explanation.

Why is the transition a doping interval?

Different kz slices cross the saddle at different fillings because interlayer dispersion is finite.

Is x simply the nominal Sr content here?

The analysis uses a Luttinger count from the measured Fermi surface as its doping calibration.

Does this rule out quantum criticality everywhere?

No. It provides a band explanation for much of the LSCO heat peak and explicitly leaves other materials and residual contributions open.

Why does finite kz dispersion matter to the DOS peak?

It lets different slices cross the saddle at different fillings and turns an idealized singularity into a finite broadened response.

What does the bulk benchmark strengthen?

The comparison of Fermi volume and hopping between the film analysis and a bulk crystal, without proving all properties identical.

Why is no gap enhancement a separate result from the heat-peak calculation?

Gap spectroscopy tests superconducting excitations, while the normal thermal peak is calculated from quasiparticle DOS and occupation.
