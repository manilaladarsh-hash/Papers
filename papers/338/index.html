The whole argument in everyday language

First, see what this work is trying to explain.

This supplied PDF is another encoding of the same titanium and lanthanum NMR article explained in source 45. Its scientific text and rendered pages match, so it supplies an alternative file and preserved provenance rather than a second experiment. The shared result is that metallic relaxation slopes rise together toward the LaTiO₃ boundary without requiring a prominent new antiferromagnetic component in the measured range. The density-of-states and mass interpretation still requires coupling, carrier-density and band assumptions. Here x remains the Sr fraction. Use the primary guide for the full explanation and original-page walkthroughs, and do not count repeated figures as independent evidence.

Terms to understand before starting
  • Mott-Hubbard insulator: An interaction-driven insulating state with a charge gap between Coulomb-split d states.
  • Sr fraction x: The substitution fraction in La1−xSrxTiO3; decreasing x approaches the parent insulator.
  • Hyperfine filter: A coupling geometry that weights electronic fluctuation wavevectors differently at different nuclei.
  • EFG: Electric field gradient that couples to a nuclear quadrupole moment.

Do not compare raw rates blindly

Look for: Ti sits on the magnetic ion; La lies at a site where transferred AF fields cancel geometrically.

The local Ti nucleus couples directly to the magnetic-ion environment, while transferred fields from ordered Ti neighbors nearly cancel at La. Their different geometric weights make the two relaxation curves a useful comparison. The source calibrates that cancellation in the insulating parent. This picture does not calculate an exact form factor or prove that every metallic La coupling is identical to its parent value.

The two nuclei provide different momentum filters, allowing a more informative comparison than one relaxation curve.

Separate line shape from dynamics

Look for: A shrinking central-line width with Sr content reflects quadrupole effects; its inverse-frequency scaling helps identify the origin.

A broad central feature can arise from second-order quadrupole effects in a powder rather than a distribution of slow magnetic fields. Varying the resonance frequency tests the characteristic inverse-frequency separation before assigning a changing electric field gradient. The satellites supply related nuclear information. Test temperature and composition separately

Look for: For a fixed metallic composition, 1/T1 is linear in T; its slope grows as Sr fraction decreases toward the boundary.

Part 3

The central shared result

At fixed metallic Sr fractions, both Ti and La have approximately constant T1T. Their rate coefficients grow similarly toward the Mott boundary. With the source's geometric and hyperfine assumptions, that pattern favors enhanced metallic density of states over a prominently growing extra AF-q component. It does not establish that every finite-q correlation is absent.

Part 4

Composition convention

Both versions use La1−xSrxTiO3, with x the Sr fraction. The insulating LaTiO3 endpoint lies at x = 0, so approach from the metal runs toward small x. The companion Tokura source 341 uses the complementary La fraction in Sr1−xLaxTiO3. Translating this convention prevents a false conflict between their trends.

Translate the mathematical statement

Essential equations, meaning and assumptions

1/(T1T) ∝ Σq |A(q)|² χ″(q,ωN)/ωN

Symbols and units: A(q): hyperfine amplitude form factor; χ″: imaginary dynamical spin susceptibility; ωN: nuclear Larmor frequency. Prefactors and the paper's coupling-weight notation are suppressed.

Physical meaning: Different nuclei weight the same host dynamics differently. Comparing Ti with filtered La constrains whether a prominent AF component is needed.

PDF p. 6

T1T ≈ constant at fixed metallic x

Symbols and units: T1: nuclear spin-lattice relaxation time; T: temperature; x: Sr fraction in this source.

Physical meaning: Equivalent to 1/T1 proportional to T. The constant may change strongly with composition, so this law is compatible with mass enhancement.

PDF p. 4 · PDF p. 5

[1/(T1T)]^(1/2) ∝ N(EF) ∝ m* n0^(1/3)

Symbols and units: N(EF): renormalized DOS; m*: effective quasiparticle mass; n0: carrier density, in the simple parabolic-band interpretation.

Physical meaning: Use the square root of the rate coefficient and account for carrier density before inferring mass; coupling and correlation factors are assumed.

PDF p. 7 · PDF p. 8

ΔHquadrupole ∝ νQ²/ν0

Symbols and units: ΔHquadrupole: central-line singularity separation, with field/frequency conversion understood; νQ: quadrupole frequency; ν0: resonance frequency.

Physical meaning: Inverse-frequency scaling tests the second-order quadrupole interpretation under the stated asymmetry assumptions.
