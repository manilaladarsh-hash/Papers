9. Recoveries and the instrument reveal hidden selection

Nonexponential recovery and finite dead time expose the limits of equating one measured rate with one local environment.

The question: How can a frequency-selected bin still contain a distribution of T₁, and why does the circuit matter?

Before you start: the terms you need
  • Nominal x: Sr atoms per LSCO formula unit, used as the average hole-doping label.
  • Electric field gradient (EFG): curvature of the electric potential at a nucleus; determines the Cu quadrupole resonance.
  • Knight shift: the nuclear resonance shift caused by electronic magnetic response, with orbital and spin contributions.
  • T₁ and T₂: recovery of longitudinal nuclear magnetization and decay of transverse coherence; they answer different questions.
  • Hyperfine form factor: the wavevector-dependent weighting through which a nucleus samples electronic spin fluctuations.
  • HWHM: half width at half maximum; the thesis also defines an effective width from the second moment.

Does an excellent single-rate fit prove all nuclei have identical microscopic dynamics?

No. A finite data window and echo selection can hide distributions; the observable may still be a useful effective rate.

Why might raising Q make fast-relaxing regions harder to see?

It lengthens ringdown and dead time, so early echoes from those regions can be obscured or decay before acquisition.

A single frequency does not guarantee one rate

Appendix B contrasts an undoped recovery with the x = 0.16 recovery at 100 K. In the latter, forcing one exponential leaves systematic curvature on a semilogarithmic plot. Singer models the normalized recovery as a positive weighted sum of exponentials with different rates. This is evidence that one selected frequency bin contains a range of dynamical environments, even after the overall frequency dependence has been measured. The reason is that a resonance EFG is not a unique local-hole label: different lattice configurations can bring nuclei with different electronic responses to the same frequency. The fitted rate distributions use a specified asymmetric parametric family and a center constraint. They illustrate this hidden mixture; they should not be interpreted as a unique unconstrained inverse transform of perfect data.

A consistency check with its own censoring

The width of a fitted rate distribution is converted to an equivalent frequency width using the local derivative of the rate-versus-frequency curve. Averaging several positions gives a lattice width around 0.62 ± 0.09 MHz in the source analysis, compared with about 0.49 MHz from the separate point-charge calculation. The agreement is a useful scale check rather than exact equality. Both the fitted mean and width can be biased because nuclei with large transverse decay and correlated fast longitudinal relaxation contribute less at finite echo spacing. The thesis explains that these effects can shift numerator and denominator of the width conversion in different directions. Measuring a good recovery over several decades is therefore necessary, and using the shortest practical τ helps, but neither action eliminates selection. The observable remains an echo-weighted rate distribution.

Quality factor trades sensitivity for early access

Appendix C explains why that τ cannot simply be set to zero. A high-Q tuned circuit enhances signal and RF field but rings down for longer after strong excitation, obscuring early echoes. Phase cycling suppresses ringdown without making it perfectly absent. For the described probe, Q is typically around 50–60; the shortest usable pulse spacing is about 12 μs. Tuning and matching maintain resonance and approximately 50 Ω input impedance across the spectral sweep. A parallel damping resistor reduces Q and its temperature dependence, helping avoid artificial intensity changes that could otherwise mimic wipeout. Filling factor, stray inductance and capacitance also affect signal and tuning range. These are scientific limits because the instrument defines which fast-decaying nuclei can be observed, not merely engineering details disconnected from interpretation.

What the evidence supports—and where it stops

  • Parametric multiexponential recovery fits are not unique rate reconstructions.
  • Probe values and dead time describe this apparatus; they are not universal NQR settings.
