The samples and the central observation

This transverse-field muon study examines LSCO crystals with x = 0.15, 0.19, 0.25 and 0.30. External fields from 0.2 to 7 T are applied mainly along the c axis, with an additional x = 0.30 crystal oriented to test an in-plane field. A nearby calcium-carbonate reference independently calibrates the applied field. On cooling, the muon precession loses amplitude more rapidly: a distribution of local fields becomes broader. Fourier transforms reveal substantial symmetric broadening but very little displacement of the mean. This matters because the overdoped region was often treated as a uniform Fermi liquid, while the observed response varies strongly from one nominally equivalent local site to another.

What the fitting parameters mean

The asymmetry is fitted by an oscillatory cosine multiplied by a fixed Gaussian decay and an additional exponential. The frequency tracks the mean field. The fixed Gaussian term accounts for nuclear dipoles and apparatus field variation; the exponential rate λ characterizes the extra field-induced width. The paper notes that a root exponential better represents some low-temperature shapes and that the usual fit becomes imperfect for x = 0.30 below 10 K. Longitudinal-field results from related measurements constrain relaxation from transverse fluctuations, supporting the interpretation of the transverse-field decay as a largely static local distribution under the measurement conditions. Relaxation is therefore not automatically a direct spin fluctuation rate.

Field, temperature and doping trends

The additional rate is approximately linear in applied field. Its slope grows on cooling and can be summarized by C(x)/(T - Θ(x)) plus a temperature-independent background. The authors emphasize that other temperature functions can describe the data equally well; a good Curie-Weiss fit is a characterization rather than a unique theory. Across the combined dataset, C grows approximately with Sr concentration. Θ changes markedly near optimal doping. The x = 0.15 crystal has a somewhat larger response than x = 0.19, so the result is not a perfectly monotonic sequence at every condition. At high overdoping, the increasing field response continues while superconducting Tc decreases.

Why the width is not simply a distribution of bulk dopings

For x = 0.30 at 2 K and 5 T the source reports an approximately 5 mT full width, while the mean is within 0.5 mT of the applied field. The size of the local distribution exceeds what shifting among known LSCO bulk doping responses would easily explain. Consequently, a patch model of different hole concentrations alone is incomplete here. A superconducting-patch explanation that correlated with Tc in underdoped samples also does not straightforwardly explain why the overdoped broadening grows as Tc weakens. These are tests of particular mechanisms, not a rejection of electronic inhomogeneity itself or a quantitative measurement of local hole concentration.

The proposed staggered response and its uncertainty

The authors explore emergent isolated moments and weak ferromagnetic clusters using moments constrained by bulk magnetization. These models have difficulty reproducing both the large local relaxation and the marked field-direction anisotropy. A small staggered cluster around a Sr dopant offers a qualitative alternative: opposite spins give large nearby fields while partially cancelling the total moment. The paper suggests that dopant disorder and magnetic field stabilize such regions, and that shrinking correlations can reconcile increasing uniform Curie response with reduced antiferromagnetic neutron weight. It explicitly calls for field- and disorder-dependent neutron studies. Keep the secure observation, spatially heterogeneous field response, distinct from the proposed microscopic staggered-island explanation.

How to compare the evidence

A useful reading exercise is to imagine two distributions with the same mean field: one narrow and one broad. Their bulk magnetizations can look similar, but implanted muons precess at a wider spread of frequencies in the second case and lose ensemble polarization faster. The experiment measures precisely this distinction, with strong orientation dependence providing another constraint. It does not follow that every broadening source is a spontaneous ferromagnet or that the proposed islands have been photographed. Compare the temperature dependence, field dependence and anisotropy jointly before choosing a microscopic model. In particular, a Curie-Weiss-like fit to the width describes a growing response; it cannot, by itself, specify the spatial pattern or establish a zero-field ordering transition.

Translate the mathematics Key equations and symbol meanings

A(t) = A0 cos(ωμt + φ) exp(-σ²t²) exp(-λt)

Symbols: A0: initial asymmetry; ωμ: average precession angular frequency; φ: phase; σ: fixed Gaussian width; λ: additional relaxation rate.

Different terms separate mean field and broadening within this chosen fit; λ is not by itself a microscopic fluctuation frequency.

PDF p. 2

(1/μ0) dλ/dH = C(x)/(T - Θ(x)) + B(x)

Symbols: H: external field; C: fitted Curie-like strength; Θ: fitted temperature offset; B: fitted background.

Describes the temperature evolution of field response; other functions fit comparably well.

Explain it back.

Try answering aloud before opening the explanation.

Why does a transverse-field result not contradict the zero-field null study?

The applied field can create or stabilize a response absent in zero field.

Is a Curie-Weiss fit unique evidence of isolated moments?

No. The paper notes alternative temperature fits and tests isolated-moment models separately.

Why can staggered islands reconcile local and bulk probes?

Opposite local contributions can partially cancel in bulk magnetization while retaining sizable local fields.
