A common language for the papers

Translate the measurement before the conclusion.

NMR / NQR

NMR observes nuclear resonances in an applied magnetic field. NQR uses electric-quadrupole splittings, usually without an applied field. The nucleus senses its local environment.

See it in a source lesson →
Knight shift

A resonance shift contains a spin-dependent static response, plus orbital and other corrections. Different couplings can cancel a spin contribution.

See it in a source lesson →
T₁ and T₂

T₁ describes longitudinal recovery; T₂ describes transverse echo decay. A fast T₂ can hide a population even when its nuclei still exist.

See it in a source lesson →
Momentum filter

A nuclear site weights the host spin spectrum through its coupling geometry. Two probes can differ without requiring two different hosts.

See it in a source lesson →
Wipeout

Loss of detectable nuclear intensity within a measurement window. Its relation to stripe order needs timing, intensity corrections and complementary evidence.

See it in a source lesson →
Nematic versus stripe

Nematic order selects a direction. Stripe order also selects a spatial modulation. A lattice can impose an orienting field before electronic order is considered.

See it in a source lesson →
Pairing versus coherence

A pair-amplitude scale, in-plane phase coherence and three-dimensional locking can differ. One transition need not establish all three.

See it in a source lesson →
Dimer singlet

Two spins can form total spin zero while retaining entanglement. A gapped singlet response does not by itself identify which exchange bonds are dominant.

See it in a source lesson →
Pseudogap

Suppression of an observable’s low-energy response above superconductivity. Specify whether the evidence is spin, heat capacity or another channel.

See it in a source lesson →
Mott versus band transition

Interaction-driven localization and a change in Fermi-surface topology involve different mechanisms. A mass estimate depends on the assumed band and carrier count.

See it in a source lesson →
Muon time window

A local magnetic field may precess or depolarize a muon spin. A static-field null applies to a particular sensitivity, geometry and fluctuation window.

See it in a source lesson →
Specific heat and entropy

Electronic C/T must be separated from other contributions and integrated over temperature to obtain entropy. Condensation energy uses an entropy difference.

Single-T₂ teaching model. Real pulse bandwidth, distributions, stretching, corrections and instrument dead time also matter. Echo loss does not directly equal a charge-order amplitude.

What can a resistivity curve establish?

Compare a linear term with a quadratic term. A similar-looking total curve can hide different contributions.

ρ = ρ₀ + aT + bT². The linear term and quadratic term add; ρ alone does not identify a unique scattering mechanism.

ρ−ρ₀ = 0.60T + 0.40T² in reduced teaching units.

Reduced T, arbitrary resistivity units. These are exact chosen functions, not a fit to LSCO data or a measurement of a quasiparticle lifetime.
